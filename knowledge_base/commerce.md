# Commerce Knowledge Base

## Exam Answer Pattern

Commerce answers should define the business term, explain its purpose, give a
real-life example, and include advantages, disadvantages, or accounting effects
when relevant.

## Business Basics

Business is an economic activity that provides goods or services to satisfy
human needs and wants, usually with the aim of earning profit.

Needs are essential for living. Wants are desires that improve comfort or
satisfaction. Goods are tangible items. Services are intangible activities.

Entrepreneurship means identifying opportunities, organizing resources, taking
risk, and creating value through a business.

## Production And Trade

Production creates utility. Land, labour, capital, and entrepreneurship are
factors of production. Trade involves buying and selling goods and services.

Home trade happens within a country. Foreign trade happens between countries.
Imports are goods brought into the country. Exports are goods sold to another
country.

Retailers sell goods to final consumers. Wholesalers buy in bulk from producers
and sell smaller quantities to retailers.

## Accounting Basics

Assets are resources owned by a business. Liabilities are amounts owed. Capital
is the owner's investment. The accounting equation is: Assets = Capital +
Liabilities.

Income increases capital. Expenses decrease capital. Profit is income greater
than expenses. Loss is expenses greater than income.

Double-entry bookkeeping records every transaction with debit and credit
effects. Source documents provide evidence for transactions.

## Banking And Money

Money is a medium of exchange, measure of value, store of value, and standard
for deferred payment. Banks accept deposits, lend money, transfer funds, and
provide payment services.

## Common Problem Templates

Definition question:
Write the term, simple meaning, purpose, and example.

Accounting equation:
Identify assets, liabilities, and capital, then apply Assets = Capital +
Liabilities.

Business comparison:
Compare ownership, control, capital, risk, profit sharing, and legal position.
