# English Knowledge Base

## Exam Answer Pattern

English answers should be clear, grammatically correct, and directly connected
to the question. For comprehension, use evidence from the passage. For writing,
plan before writing and check tense, punctuation, spelling, and paragraphing.

## Grammar

Parts of speech:
- Noun: names a person, place, thing, or idea.
- Verb: shows action or state.
- Adjective: describes a noun.
- Adverb: describes a verb, adjective, or another adverb.
- Pronoun: replaces a noun.
- Preposition: shows relationship, place, direction, or time.
- Conjunction: joins words, phrases, or clauses.

Subject-verb agreement means the verb must match the subject. Singular subjects
usually take singular verbs. Example: She goes. Plural subjects take plural
verbs. Example: They go.

Tenses:
- Simple present: habits and general truths.
- Present continuous: actions happening now.
- Simple past: completed past actions.
- Past continuous: action continuing in the past.
- Present perfect: past action connected to the present.
- Future: actions that will happen.

Active voice: The student wrote the answer. Passive voice: The answer was
written by the student. Use passive voice when the action or object is more
important than the doer.

Direct speech reports exact words. Indirect speech reports meaning. Change
pronouns, tense, and time expressions when converting.

## Comprehension

Read the questions before reading the passage. Underline key words. For
literal questions, find the direct answer. For inference questions, combine
clues from the passage with logical thinking. For vocabulary questions, use
context before choosing meaning.

Good comprehension answers are short, complete, and based on the passage.
Avoid adding outside information unless the question asks for your opinion.

## Writing

Paragraph structure:
Topic sentence, supporting details, example or explanation, closing sentence.

Essay structure:
Introduction, body paragraphs in logical order, conclusion. Keep one main idea
per paragraph.

Letter writing:
Use the correct format. Formal letters need sender address, date, receiver
address, greeting, subject, body, closing, and signature. Informal letters use
a friendly tone and personal details.

Speech writing:
Use greeting, introduce topic, organize points, use direct address, and end
with thanks or a memorable closing.

Notice writing:
Include event, date, time, venue, purpose, organizer, and contact if needed.

## Vocabulary

Synonyms have similar meanings. Antonyms have opposite meanings. Prefixes come
before root words and can change meaning. Suffixes come after root words and
can change word class.

## Common Problem Templates

Grammar correction:
Identify the error type, write the corrected sentence, and check the whole
sentence again.

Comprehension answer:
Find evidence, write the answer in your own words, and keep it direct.

Essay:
Plan three main points, write one paragraph per point, and connect ideas with
linking words.
